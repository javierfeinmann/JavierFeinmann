Higher Education around the World · release v1.0 · September 2026

Documentation

Who controls the most internationally prominent universities of each country, and what does a domestic student pay to attend them? This dataset codes the top five universities of 55 countries and territories in the QS World University Rankings 2024 — 274 institutions — for control (public or private), the standard annual charge to a domestic first-degree student relative to GDP per capita, and the existence of means-tested support, by who funds it and how it is paid. Every institution is backed by documented sources (3,083 in total), each with its URL, access date and a verbatim quote.

How to cite. If you use these data, please cite:

Feinmann, Javier and Roberto Hsu Rocha. “Inequality and Higher Education: The Role of Elite Public Colleges.” Working paper. [PDF]

and, where useful, the release: Higher Education around the World dataset, v1.0 (September 2026).

Corrections, missing sources and suggestions are very welcome: jfeinmann@berkeley.edu.

1. Sample

For each country, the five highest-ranked institutions in the QS World University Rankings 2024 (published 27 June 2023), fixed before any price was collected. All institutions tied at the fifth position are included, and countries with fewer than five ranked institutions keep all they have. The sample is therefore internationally ranked research universities, not the universe of nationally selective programmes: in some countries (for example Argentina, Mexico and Peru) small private universities rank above large public ones.

The countries were chosen by the investigators to cover every world region and a range of higher-education models; they are not a random sample of countries. Hong Kong SAR is a separate unit; Taiwan is included; Macau is not. Countries and number of institutions:

Argentina (5), Australia (5), Austria (5), Belgium (5), Brazil (5), Canada (5), Chile (5), China (5), Colombia (5), Croatia (4), Czechia (5), Denmark (5), Ecuador (7), Egypt (8), Estonia (3), Finland (5), France (5), Germany (5), Greece (5), Hong Kong (5), Hungary (5), India (5), Indonesia (5), Ireland (5), Israel (5), Italy (5), Japan (5), Lithuania (5), Malaysia (5), Mexico (5), Netherlands (5), New Zealand (5), Nigeria (2), Norway (5), Pakistan (5), Paraguay (1), Peru (7), Poland (5), Portugal (5), Romania (10), Saudi Arabia (5), Singapore (4), Slovenia (3), South Africa (5), South Korea (5), Spain (5), Sweden (5), Switzerland (5), Taiwan (5), Thailand (5), Turkey (5), Ukraine (6), United Kingdom (5), United States (5), Uruguay (4).

2. What is measured

Reference student and year. A citizen, locally resident, full-time student in a first undergraduate degree, at a standard load, in academic year 2023/24 (or calendar 2023). Where a source for that year could not be found and the same regime clearly applied, the nearest year is used and flagged (17 institutions carry a year label other than 2023/24 or calendar 2023; see year_observed).

Control (strict). An institution is public if the state owns it (a public-law body created by the state, with no private founder) or appoints a majority of its governing body; otherwise it is private (non-profit or for-profit). Public funding and academic autonomy do not enter this definition. Separately, a government-dependence flag marks private institutions that receive substantial public funds explicitly for tuition or teaching (for example per-student teaching grants or the state paying students' fees). Research funding and student loans do not count.

Headline charge. Standard annual tuition plus compulsory recurring fees paid by every reference student, after reductions that apply to all students in every year of study, and before any targeted aid. Housing, health insurance, application and entrance-exam fees are excluded. Where prices vary by programme, the headline is the midpoint of the published range (minimum and maximum are kept); where they depend on family income, it is the full-pay rate. Reductions limited to the first year are not part of the headline.

Price bands. The headline charge divided by GDP per capita 2023 in current local currency (World Bank; for Taiwan, the official national accounts), grouped in bands fixed before coding: free < 1%, low 1–5%, low–moderate 5–10%, moderate 10–25%, high ≥ 25%. An alternative set of cut-offs (2.5 / 5 / 15 / 30%) is reported as a sensitivity check.

Means-tested support. Non-repayable support whose eligibility depends on household or parental income, coded in four cells: funded by the government or run by the institution, times paid as a fee reduction (waiver, income-scaled fee, or grant paid against the charge) or as cash to the student (for example living-cost grants). Each cell is yes, no (searched, not found), unknown, or not_applicable (a fee reduction where the charge is zero). Loans, in-kind support and merit-only awards are excluded. Aid is never subtracted from the headline charge. Where documented, the share of students receiving each instrument is recorded.

Enrolment. One figure per institution for 2023/24 or 2023: first-degree headcount where published (201 institutions), otherwise undergraduate headcount, total headcount or full-time equivalents (flagged; 67 institutions). Found for 268 of 274 institutions. Enrolment is used only for weighting within countries.

3. Country summaries

3×3 grid. Institutions are placed by control (public; private and government-dependent; private and not government-dependent) and price (free; low to low–moderate; moderate or high). A country is placed in the cell that holds the largest number of its priced institutions (unweighted) or the largest share of their enrolment (enrolment-weighted); a country tied between two cells counts half in each. Institutions without an established price are left out.

Country type (rule fixed in advance). Control type from the share of public institutions: public if at least 0.7 (4 or 5 of 5), mixed if 0.3 to 0.7, private below 0.3. Price type is the median band; it is marked incomplete when a missing price could move the median. Each country type is recomputed under several alternative definitions (top 1 and top 3 only; alternative band cut-offs; government-dependent private counted as public; enrolment weights; the low and high ends of programme ranges; pricing the free track where free places are rationed by exam score; setting the price to zero where a documented majority of entrants pay nothing). These are the sens_* columns of countries.csv.

Weights. Summary shares are shown pooled (each institution counts once), per country (country shares averaged with equal weight) and per country, enrolment-weighted. The association with income inequality uses the World Bank Poverty and Inequality Platform Gini (latest national survey 2013–2023) and is descriptive only.

4. How the data were collected and checked

Collection. The data were collected with the help of AI research agents (Anthropic's Claude) working under a written protocol set by the investigators. Collectors were given the definitions and the distinctions to keep separate, and were never told what result to expect for a country. Every fact had to be supported by an opened source: the exact URL, a verbatim quote in the original language, a locator and the access date. Where a live page had changed since 2023/24, contemporaneous archived captures (Internet Archive) were used. Unknown is recorded as unknown: a missing price or a missing grant is never read as zero.

Independent check. Each institution was checked once by a second agent working in a fresh context, which re-opened the cited sources (and others where needed) and asked whether they give the same control and the same price band. Exact figures need not match; categories must. Control agreed for 274 of 274 institutions and the band for 265; the exceptions are listed in section 6. Disagreements were resolved with evidence or left unresolved, and every correction was applied by script, so all tables can be rebuilt from the recorded evidence. The checker is the same kind of model as the collector: a fresh context reduces anchoring, but errors need not be independent.

Human review. The investigators set the protocol and made every methodological decision (section 5), reviewed contested cases and commissioned a skeptical review of the country-level conclusions. Not every individual value has been re-verified by hand. That is one reason every source is published: anyone can follow a coding back to the page it rests on.

6. Limitations and known issues

  • Which universities. The sample is defined by an international research ranking. It says nothing directly about the rest of each system (non-ranked public universities, vocational and short-cycle institutions), where most students often study. Five institutions per country is a small window.
  • Which countries. 55 countries and territories chosen by the investigators, not a random sample. Some countries have fewer than five ranked institutions (Paraguay 1, Nigeria 2, Estonia 3, Slovenia 3, Croatia 4, Singapore 4, Uruguay 4) and some more because of ties at the fifth position (Ukraine 6, Ecuador 7, Peru 7, Egypt 8, Romania 10).
  • Sticker price, not net price. The headline charge is what a student pays before targeted aid. For low-income students at institutions with generous means-tested aid (for example elite US private universities) the net price can be far lower. Aid is coded as present or absent, not by generosity; coverage shares are recorded only where documented. 404 of the 274×4 aid cells are unknown, so shares of institutions with aid are lower bounds.
  • One reference student. Charges for international students, part-time students, second degrees and separately priced tracks are not covered.
  • Programme variation. Where prices vary by programme, the midpoint can sit in a different band from the cheapest or most expensive programmes (band_unstable, and the low-end / high-end sensitivities).
  • Denominator. GDP per capita in current local currency. For Ireland it is inflated by multinational accounting, which lowers Irish ratios; purchasing-power comparisons across countries are not the aim.
  • Close to a cut-off. 36 institutions sit within 10% (relative) of a band threshold, so a small change in price or GDP would move them.

  • Contested control.   • Open items in this release.   • Enrolment. Measures differ across institutions (first-degree, undergraduate, total headcount or FTE), and some counts are course enrolments rather than students. Enrolment is missing for 274−268 institutions, which removes Ecuador and Pakistan from weighted results.
  • Inequality. The Gini comparison is an association across a small number of countries with strong regional clustering, not an effect. Consumption-based Ginis (China, Egypt, India, Indonesia, Nigeria, Pakistan, South Africa, Thailand, Ukraine) are lower than income-based ones; Hong Kong, New Zealand, Saudi Arabia and Singapore have no survey on the platform; Argentina has only an urban survey.
  • Quotes. Source quotes longer than 400 characters are shortened in this release (751 quotes, marked in quote_truncated); the URLs lead to the full text.
